## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Cell Theory & History**: Discoveries by Robert Hooke (1665), Anton van Leeuwenhoek, Robert Brown, Purkinje, Schleiden & Schwann, and Rudolf Virchow.
* **Structural Organization of a Cell**: Plasma membrane, Cell wall, Nucleus, and Cytoplasm.
* **Membrane Transport**: Diffusion and Osmosis; cell behavior in Hypotonic, Isotonic, and Hypertonic solutions; Endocytosis and Plasmolysis.
* **Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells**: Structural, genetic, and organelle differences.
* **Cell Organelles & Their Specialized Functions**:
  * Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER & SER, membrane biogenesis)
  * Golgi Apparatus (packaging, secretion, lysosome formation)
  * Lysosomes ("suicide bags", autolysis)
  * Mitochondria ("powerhouses", ATP generation, semi-autonomous)
  * Plastids (chloroplasts, chromoplasts, leucoplasts)
  * Vacuoles (turgidity, waste management, sap storage)
* **Plant Cell vs. Animal Cell**: Distinct comparative features.
* **Cell Division Overview**: Mitosis (growth/repair, identical diploid daughter cells) vs. Meiosis (gamete formation, haploid daughter cells).

## 2. Cell Discovery Milestones

| Scientist | Year | Landmark Discovery |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Robert Hooke** | 1665 | Observed dead cork cells under self-designed primitive microscope; coined the term **"Cell"** (little rooms). |
| **Anton van Leeuwenhoek** | 1674 | Discovered free-living **living cells** (bacteria, protozoa) in pond water with improved microscope. |
| **Robert Brown** | 1831 | Discovered and named the **Nucleus** in plant cells. |
| **J.E. Purkinje** | 1839 | Coined the term **"Protoplasm"** for the fluid substance of the cell. |
| **Schleiden (1838) & Schwann (1839)** | — | Proposed the **Classical Cell Theory**: All plants and animals are composed of cells; the cell is the fundamental unit of life. |
| **Rudolf Virchow** | 1855 | Expanded cell theory: *"Omnis cellula-e cellula"* — **All cells arise from pre-existing cells** by division. |

## 3. Structural Organization: Boundary & Transport

### A. Plasma Membrane (Cell Membrane)
* **Structure**: Flexible, ultra-thin boundary composed of lipids and proteins (Fluid Mosaic model).
* **Function**: **Selectively permeable membrane** — permits entry and exit of certain selective substances while preventing others.
* **Endocytosis**: Flexibility of membrane allows amoeba to engulf food from external environment.

### B. Osmosis & Tonicity Solutions
* **Osmosis**: The net diffusion of water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to lower water concentration.

| Solution Type | Relative Concentration | What Happens to Cell? | Scientific Term |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypotonic Solution** | External medium has **higher water concentration** (more dilute) than cell. | Water enters cell by **endosmosis**. | Cell **swells up** (plant cells become turgid; animal cells may burst). |
| **Isotonic Solution** | External medium has **exact same water concentration** as cell. | No net movement of water. | Cell size **remains unchanged**. |
| **Hypertonic Solution**| External medium has **lower water concentration** (more concentrated/salty). | Water leaves cell by **exosmosis**. | Cell **shrinks** (plant cell protoplast shrinks away from cell wall = **Plasmolysis**). |

### C. Cell Wall
* Present **only in plant cells, fungi, and bacteria** (outside plasma membrane).
* Composed of tough, fibrous **cellulose** providing structural strength, rigidity, and protection against osmotic bursting.

## 4. Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells

| Characteristic | Prokaryotic Cell (e.g. Bacteria, Cyanobacteria) | Eukaryotic Cell (e.g. Amoeba, Onion cell, Human cheek cell) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | Small ($1 - 10\ \mu\text{m}$) | Larger ($5 - 100\ \mu\text{m}$) |
| **Nuclear Region** | Poorly defined; lacks nuclear membrane; undefined region called **Nucleoid**. | Well-defined with double-layered **nuclear membrane**. |
| **Chromosome** | Single, circular DNA | Multiple linear chromosomes associated with histone proteins. |
| **Membrane-bound Organelles** | **Absent** (no mitochondria, plastids, Golgi, ER) | **Present** (mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, etc.) |
| **Ribosomes** | 70S ribosomes | 80S ribosomes (cytoplasm), 70S (inside mitochondria/plastids) |

## 5. Master Guide to Cell Organelles

```
                                  Cell Organelle Hub
  ┌───────────────────────┬───────────────┴───────────────┬───────────────────────┐
  │                       │                               │                       │
Endoplasmic Reticulum   Golgi Apparatus               Mitochondria            Plastids (Plants)
• RER: Ribosomes,       • Cisternae stacks            • "Powerhouse"          • Chloroplast: Green (Photosyn.)
  Protein synthesis     • Packaging & dispatch        • ATP synthesis         • Chromoplast: Colored pigments
• SER: Lipid synthesis, • Forms Lysosomes             • Cristae folds         • Leucoplast: Starch/protein storage
  Detoxifies poisons                                  • Own DNA & ribosomes   • Own DNA & ribosomes
```

### 1. Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)
* **Rough ER (RER)**: Surface studded with **ribosomes**; site of **protein synthesis**.
* **Smooth ER (SER)**: Lacks ribosomes; synthesizes **lipids and fats**. In liver cells of vertebrates, SER detoxifies drugs and poisons.
* **Membrane Biogenesis**: The process where proteins and lipids synthesized by RER and SER are used for building the plasma membrane.

### 2. Golgi Apparatus (Camillo Golgi)
* Stacks of membrane-bound flattened sacs called **cisternae**.
* Functions: Storage, modification, and packaging of products in vesicles; complex sugar synthesis from simple sugars; involved in **formation of lysosomes**.

### 3. Lysosomes ("Suicide Bags")
* Membrane-bound sacs filled with powerful hydrolytic digestive enzymes synthesized by RER.
* Functions: Waste disposal system of the cell; digests foreign pathogens and worn-out cellular organelles.
* *Why "Suicide Bags"?* When cell metabolism is damaged or cell dies, lysosomes burst and their released enzymes digest their own parent cell.

### 4. Mitochondria ("Powerhouses of the Cell")
* Has **two membranes**: outer porous membrane, and inner membrane deeply folded into **cristae** (increases surface area for ATP-generating chemical reactions).
* Synthesizes **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)** — the chemical energy currency of the cell.
* **Semi-autonomous**: Mitochondria possess their **own DNA and ribosomes**, enabling them to synthesize some of their own proteins.

### 5. Plastids (Present Only in Plants)
* **Chloroplasts**: Contain green pigment **chlorophyll**; essential for **photosynthesis**.
* **Chromoplasts**: Contain red/yellow/orange carotenoid pigments; give colors to flowers and fruits to attract pollinators.
* **Leucoplasts**: Colorless plastids that store starch, oils, and protein granules.
* Like mitochondria, plastids have their **own DNA and ribosomes**.

### 6. Vacuoles
* Plant cells have a **single, huge central vacuole** (occupies $50\% - 90\%$ of cell volume) filled with cell sap; provides **turgidity and rigidity**.
* Animal cells have small, temporary vacuoles.
* In freshwater unicellular organisms like *Amoeba*, contractile vacuoles expel excess water and wastes.

## 6. Plant Cell vs. Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Wall** | Present (Cellulose) | Absent |
| **Plastids** | Present (Chloroplasts, etc.) | Absent |
| **Vacuoles** | Large, permanent central vacuole | Small, temporary vacuoles |
| **Centrosome / Centrioles** | Absent (except primitive plants) | Present (aids cell division) |

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Who proposed that all cells arise from pre-existing cells? *(Answer: Rudolf Virchow in 1855)*
2. What will happen to an animal cell placed in a hypertonic solution? *(Answer: It will lose water by exosmosis and shrink)*
3. Which two cell organelles have their own DNA and ribosomes? *(Answer: Mitochondria and Plastids)*
4. Name the organelle responsible for detoxifying poisons and drugs in vertebrate liver cells. *(Answer: Smooth Endoplasmic Reticulum / SER)*
5. Why is the inner membrane of mitochondria deeply folded? *(Answer: To create a large surface area for ATP-generating chemical reactions)*

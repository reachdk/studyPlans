## 2. Atmospheric Climate Control & Winds

* **Role of Atmosphere**:
  * Acts as a protective blanket: Air is a **poor conductor of heat**, preventing sudden temperature spikes during the day and preventing heat from escaping into outer space at night (unlike the Moon where temperature ranges from $-190^\circ\text{C}$ to $+110^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Wind Generation & Coastal Breezes**:
  * **Sea Breeze (Daytime)**: Land heats up faster than water. Air above land gets hot and rises, creating low pressure. Cooler, denser air over the sea moves toward land.
  * **Land Breeze (Nighttime)**: Land cools down faster than water. Air above the warm sea rises, causing cooler air from the land to blow toward the sea.

### 1. The Nitrogen Cycle (Step-by-Step)
Nitrogen constitutes $78\%$ of atmospheric gas, essential for proteins, nucleic acids (DNA/RNA), ATP, and vitamins, but plants cannot absorb elemental $\text{N}_2$ directly.
1. **Nitrogen Fixation**: Conversion of atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ into usable nitrogenous compounds (ammonia/nitrates) by:
   * Symbiotic bacteria (*Rhizobium* in root nodules of leguminous plants like peas, beans).
   * Free-living soil bacteria (*Azotobacter*) and blue-green algae (*Anabaena*, *Nostoc*).
   * High temperature/pressure during lightning discharges.
2. **Nitrification**: Ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) is oxidized into nitrites ($\text{NO}_2^-$) by *Nitrosomonas*, then into nitrates ($\text{NO}_3^-$) by *Nitrobacter*.
3. **Assimilation**: Plants absorb nitrates from soil to synthesize plant amino acids and proteins; animals obtain nitrogen by eating plants.
4. **Ammonification**: Decomposers (bacteria/fungi) break down nitrogenous waste and dead organic matter back into ammonia.
5. **Denitrification**: Denitrifying bacteria (e.g. *Pseudomonas*, *Thiobacillus*) convert soil nitrates back into free elemental atmospheric $\text{N}_2$ gas, maintaining chemical equilibrium.

### 2. The Carbon Cycle
* Carbon moves through both living (biotic) and non-living (abiotic) realms:
  * **Fixation**: Green plants fix atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ into glucose via **photosynthesis**.
  * **Release**: Cellular **respiration** in plants and animals returns $\text{CO}_2$ to atmosphere; decomposition and combustion of fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) release stored carbon.
  * **Sinks**: Vast amounts of carbon are dissolved as ocean carbonates and incorporated into limestone rocks and marine shell skeletons.

## 4. Global Environmental Issues

### A. Greenhouse Effect & Global Warming
* Certain trace atmospheric gases—principally **Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)**, Methane ($\text{CH}_4$), Nitrous Oxide ($\text{N}_2\text{O}$), and water vapor—trap reflected infrared (heat) radiation from Earth's surface.
* An enhanced greenhouse effect due to deforestation and excessive fossil fuel combustion causes **Global Warming**, resulting in melting polar ice sheets, sea level rise, and severe climate disruptions.

### B. Ozone Layer Depletion ($O_3$)
* **Location**: Stratosphere ($15 - 30\text{ km}$ above ground).
* **Vital Function**: Absorbs hazardous high-energy **ultraviolet (UV) radiation** from the Sun, preventing skin cancers, eye cataracts, and genetic mutations in living organisms.
* **The Culprit**: **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)** used in aerosol sprays, refrigerants, and air conditioning. In the stratosphere, UV breaks CFCs to release reactive chlorine radicals that catalyze the breakdown of thousands of ozone molecules ($O_3 \rightarrow O_2 + O$).
* **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: International treaty successfully banning the production of CFCs worldwide.

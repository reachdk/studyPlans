## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Need for Classification**: Over 1.8 million discovered species require systematic organization based on shared evolutionary ancestry and structural characteristics.
* **Basis of Classification**:
  * Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic cellular design.
  * Unicellular vs. Multicellular organization.
  * Autotrophic (Photosynthetic) vs. Heterotrophic nutrition.
  * Specialized organ systems and body symmetry.
* **Taxonomic Hierarchy (Linnaeus)**: Kingdom $\rightarrow$ Phylum (Animals) / Division (Plants) $\rightarrow$ Class $\rightarrow$ Order $\rightarrow$ Family $\rightarrow$ Genus $\rightarrow$ Species.
* **Five Kingdom Classification (Robert H. Whittaker, 1969)**: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
* **Plant Kingdom Breakdown**: Thallophyta (Algae), Bryophyta (Amphibians of plant kingdom), Pteridophyta, Gymnosperms (naked seeds), Angiosperms (enclosed seeds in fruits: Monocots & Dicots).
* **Animal Kingdom Phyla**: Porifera, Coelenterata (Cnidaria), Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda (largest), Mollusca, Echinodermata, and Chordata (Vertebrata: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia).
* **Binomial Nomenclature (Carl Linnaeus)**: Rules of scientific naming ($Genus\ species$).

## 3. Plant Kingdom (Division Breakdown)

| Division | Vascular Tissue (Xylem/Phloem) | Body Differentiation | Reproductive Habit / Seeds | Classic Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Thallophyta (Algae)** | **Absent** | Undifferentiated plant body (called **thallus**; no true root, stem, leaf). | Spores; aquatic habit. | *Spirogyra*, *Ulothrix*, *Cladophora*, *Chara*. |
| **2. Bryophyta** | **Absent** | Body differentiated into stem-like and leaf-like structures (no true roots, anchored by **rhizoids**). | Spores; **"Amphibians of plant kingdom"** because they live on land but require water for fertilization. | *Funaria* (Moss), *Marchantia*, *Riccia*. |
| **3. Pteridophyta** | **Present** (Vascular bundles) | Well-differentiated into true roots, stems, and leaves. | Produce hidden spores (**Cryptogams**); do not bear flowers or seeds. | *Marsilea*, Ferns, Horsetails. |
| **4. Gymnosperms** | **Present** | Differentiated woody perennials, evergreen trees. | Bear **naked seeds** not enclosed in fruit; male and female cones; non-flowering. | *Pinus* (Pine), *Cycas*, Deodar. |
| **5. Angiosperms** | **Present** | Highly developed flowering plants. | Seeds developed inside an ovary that matures into a **fruit** (**Phanerogams**). | Mango, Pea, Maize, Mustard. |

### Angiosperm Subgroups: Monocots vs. Dicots
* **Monocotyledons (Monocots)**: Single cotyledon seed, fibrous root system, parallel leaf venation, trimerous flowers (e.g. Maize, Wheat, Grass, Rice).
* **Dicotyledons (Dicots)**: Two cotyledons in seed, tap root system, reticulate (net-like) leaf venation, tetramerous/pentamerous flowers (e.g. Pea, Gram, Mustard, Sunflower).

## 4. Animal Kingdom Phyla Summary

| Phylum | Organization & Symmetry | Coelom (Body Cavity) | Defining Special Characteristics | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Porifera** | Cellular level, asymmetrical | Acoelomate | Non-motile, pore-bearing body (**Ostia & Osculum**), internal canal system. | *Sycon*, *Spongilla*, *Euplectella*. |
| **2. Coelenterata (Cnidaria)**| Tissue level, **radial** | Acoelomate | Aquatic, gastrovascular cavity with single mouth opening, stinging tentacles (**Cnidoblasts**). | *Hydra*, *Aurelia* (Jellyfish), Sea Anemone. |
| **3. Platyhelminthes** | Organ level, **bilateral**, triploblastic | Acoelomate | Dorso-ventrally flattened (**Flatworms**), blind sac body, flame cells for excretion. | *Planaria* (free-living), *Taenia* (Tapeworm), *Fasciola* (Liver fluke). |
| **4. Nematoda (Aschelminthes)** | Bilateral, triploblastic | **Pseudocoelomate** (false body cavity) | Cylindrical body (**Roundworms**), parasitic, complete alimentary canal. | *Ascaris* (Roundworm), *Wuchereria* (causes Elephantiasis/Filaria). |
| **5. Annelida** | Bilateral, triploblastic | **True Coelom** (Coelomate) | Metamerically segmented body (rings), nephridia for excretion, moist skin respiration. | *Pheretima* (Earthworm), *Hirudinaria* (Leech). |
| **6. Arthropoda** | Bilateral, coelomate | Haemocoel (open blood cavity) | **Largest animal phylum**; jointed appendages, chitinous exoskeleton. | Butterfly, Mosquito, Prawn, Scorpion, Spider, Crab. |
| **7. Mollusca** | Bilateral, coelomate | Coelomate | Soft unsegmented body, muscular foot for locomotion, calcareous shell, open circulatory system. | *Pila* (Apple snail), *Unio*, *Octopus*. |
| **8. Echinodermata** | Radial (adult) / Bilateral (larva) | Coelomate | Spiny-skinned, marine, **water vascular system** with tube feet for locomotion and food capture. | *Asterias* (Starfish), Sea Urchin, Sea Cucumber. |
| **9. Vertebrata (Chordates)**| Organ system level | Coelomate | Notochord replaced by cartilaginous or bony **vertebral column**; dorsal nerve cord, paired gill slits. | Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia. |

## 5. Binomial Nomenclature (Carl Linnaeus)

* **Rules for Writing Scientific Names**:
  1. The name consists of two Latin words: **Genus** (starts with a **Capital letter**) followed by **Species** (starts with a **lowercase letter**).
  2. When printed, names are in *italics* (e.g. *Homo sapiens*, *Mangifera indica*).
  3. When handwritten, Genus and species must be **underlined separately** (e.g. <u>Homo</u> <u>sapiens</u>).

## 6. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Who proposed the Five Kingdom system of classification? *(Answer: Robert H. Whittaker in 1969)*
2. Why are Bryophytes called the "Amphibians of the plant kingdom"? *(Answer: Because they inhabit terrestrial land but strictly require water for sexual reproduction and flagellated sperm swimming)*
3. What is the largest phylum of the animal kingdom characterized by jointed legs? *(Answer: Phylum Arthropoda)*
4. Name the phylum that exhibits a pseudocoelom and causes Elephantiasis. *(Answer: Phylum Nematoda — Wuchereria)*
5. What are the two leaf venation patterns characteristic of Monocots and Dicots? *(Answer: Monocots have parallel venation; Dicots have reticulate venation)*

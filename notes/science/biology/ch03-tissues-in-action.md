## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Definition of Tissue**: A group of cells with similar structure performing a specialized common function.
* **Plant vs. Animal Tissues**: Structural differences reflecting stationary plant habits vs. mobile animal locomotion.
* **Plant Tissues**:
  * **Meristematic Tissues**: Apical, Intercalary, and Lateral meristems.
  * **Permanent Tissues**: Differentiation process; Simple permanent (Parenchyma, Chlorenchyma, Aerenchyma, Collenchyma, Sclerenchyma) vs. Complex permanent (Xylem & Phloem).
  * **Protective Tissues**: Epidermis, stomata with guard cells, cutin, suberin in cork cells.
* **Animal Tissues**:
  * **Epithelial Tissue**: Squamous, cuboidal, columnar, ciliated, and glandular epithelium.
  * **Connective Tissue**: Blood, bone, cartilage, ligaments, tendons, areolar, and adipose tissue.
  * **Muscular Tissue**: Striated (skeletal/voluntary), Unstriated (smooth/involuntary), and Cardiac muscle fibers.
  * **Nervous Tissue**: Neuron anatomy (cyton, axon, dendrites, myelin sheath) and transmission of nerve impulses.

## 3. Plant Tissues: Detailed Breakdown

### A. Meristematic Tissues (Active Cell Division)
* Cells are small, thin-walled, densely packed with dense cytoplasm, prominent nuclei, and **no vacuoles** (since they do not store food and divide continuously).
1. **Apical Meristem**: Present at growing tips of stems and roots; increases **length** of plant (primary growth).
2. **Intercalary Meristem**: Located at base of leaves or internodes (e.g. grasses); increases length of organs and enables regrowth after grazing.
3. **Lateral Meristem (Cambium)**: Located along the radial sides; increases **girth / diameter** (secondary growth).

### B. Simple Permanent Tissues (Single Cell Type)
Formed when meristematic cells take up a permanent shape, size, and function through **differentiation**:

| Tissue | Cell Living Status | Cell Wall Feature | Intercellular Spaces | Specialized Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Parenchyma** | **Living**, unspecialized | Thin primary cellulose wall | **Large** intercellular spaces | Food storage and packing tissue. |
| • *Chlorenchyma* | Living | Contains chloroplasts | Present | Performs **photosynthesis** (leaves). |
| • *Aerenchyma* | Living | Large air cavities | Very large | Gives **buoyancy** to aquatic plants to float. |
| **Collenchyma** | **Living**, elongated | Irregularly thickened corners (cellulose/pectin) | **Very little / none** | Provides **mechanical flexibility** without breaking (bending in tendrils/leaf stalks). |
| **Sclerenchyma** | **Dead** at maturity, narrow & long | Extremely thick walls with **lignin** deposition | **Absent** | Provides **mechanical strength and rigidity** (coconut husk, walnut shells, pear grit). |

### C. Complex Permanent Tissues (Conducting / Vascular Tissues)
Composed of more than one type of cell working together as a unit:

| Feature | Xylem (Water Transport) | Phloem (Food Translocation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Function** | Transports water and dissolved mineral ions from roots to aerial parts. | Translocates synthesized soluble food (sucrose, amino acids) from leaves to all parts. |
| **Direction of Flow**| Strictly **Unidirectional** (upwards only). | **Bidirectional** (upwards and downwards). |
| **Components** | 1. **Tracheids** (Dead, tubular, pitted)<br>2. **Vessels** (Dead, long open tubes)<br>3. **Xylem Fibres** (Dead, supportive)<br>4. **Xylem Parenchyma** (**Living**, stores food) | 1. **Sieve Tubes** (Living, perforated sieve plates, no nucleus at maturity)<br>2. **Companion Cells** (Living, nucleated helper cells)<br>3. **Phloem Parenchyma** (Living, stores food)<br>4. **Phloem Fibres** (**Dead**, provides mechanical strength) |
| **Living vs. Dead** | **3 dead elements, 1 living** (only parenchyma is living). | **3 living elements, 1 dead** (only phloem fibres are dead). |

## 4. Master Classification: Animal Tissues

```
                               Animal Tissues
                                     │
         ┌──────────────────┬────────┴──────────┬──────────────────┐
     Epithelial         Connective           Muscular           Nervous
    (Protection)       (Binding/Support)   (Movement)          (Signaling)
```

### 1. Epithelial Tissue (Covering & Protective Boundary)
* Tightly packed sheets resting on an extracellular **basement membrane**; lacks blood vessels.
1. **Simple Squamous**: Thin, flat, delicate lining (alveoli of lungs, blood capillary lining, mouth lining); enables rapid diffusion.
2. **Stratified Squamous**: Multiple stacked layers of squamous cells (skin epidermis); prevents wear and tear.
3. **Cuboidal Epithelium**: Cube-shaped cells lining kidney tubules and salivary gland ducts; provides mechanical support and absorption.
4. **Columnar Epithelium**: Tall pillar-like cells lining the intestine; facilitates absorption and secretion.
5. **Ciliated Epithelium**: Columnar cells bearing hair-like **cilia** on free surfaces (respiratory tract); rhythmically sweeps mucus forward.
6. **Glandular Epithelium**: Infolded epithelial cells specialized for secretion (sweat, oil, enzyme glands).

### 2. Connective Tissue (Binding, Packing & Supporting Framework)
Cells loosely spaced and embedded in an intercellular **matrix**:
1. **Fluid Connective Tissue (Blood)**: Liquid plasma ($55\%$) containing suspended **RBCs** (hemoglobin, oxygen transport), **WBCs** (immunity/defense), and **Platelets** (blood clotting).
2. **Bone**: Hard, rigid matrix composed of **Calcium and Phosphorus** compounds; forms skeletal framework; contains osteocyte cells.
3. **Cartilage**: Flexible matrix composed of proteins and sugars; smooths bone surfaces at joints (nose tip, ear pinna, trachea, larynx).
4. **Ligament**: Connects **Bone to Bone**; very elastic with considerable strength and little matrix.
5. **Tendon**: Connects **Muscle to Bone**; fibrous tissue with great strength but limited flexibility.
6. **Areolar Tissue**: Found between skin and muscles, around blood vessels and nerves; fills internal spaces, supports internal organs, and repairs tissues.
7. **Adipose Tissue**: Fat-storing cells (adipocytes) located below the skin and between internal organs; acts as an **insulator against heat loss**.

### 3. Muscular Tissue (Contractile Movement)
Elongated muscle fibers containing contractile proteins (**actin and myosin**) that contract and relax to cause body motion:

| Feature | Striated (Skeletal) Muscle | Unstriated (Smooth) Muscle | Cardiac (Heart) Muscle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Control** | **Voluntary** (conscious control) | **Involuntary** (automatic control) | **Involuntary** (rhythmic automatic) |
| **Shape** | Long, cylindrical, unbranched | Spindle-shaped (tapering ends) | Cylindrical, branched |
| **Striations** | Alternate dark and light bands present | Unstriated (smooth) | Faint striations present |
| **Nuclei** | **Multinucleate** (many nuclei at periphery) | **Uninucleate** (single central nucleus) | **Uninucleate** |
| **Location** | Attached to bones (limbs, neck) | Walls of stomach, intestines, blood vessels, iris, bronchi | Heart wall only |
| **Fatigue** | Gets fatigued quickly | Does not fatigue easily | **Never fatigues** throughout life |

### 4. Nervous Tissue (High-Speed Electrical Signaling)
* Found in **Brain, Spinal Cord, and Nerves**.
* Structural and functional unit is the **Neuron** (nerve cell):
  * **Cell Body (Cyton)**: Contains nucleus and cytoplasm with Nissl granules.
  * **Dendrites**: Short, branched projections that receive incoming signals.
  * **Axon**: Long, single cylindrical projection conducting nerve impulse away from cell body to terminal endings.
  * **Synapse**: Microscopic junction between axon terminal of one neuron and dendrite of the next.

## 5. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Why do meristematic cells lack vacuoles? *(Answer: They are continuously actively dividing cells that do not need to store food or maintain large sap turgidity)*
2. What chemical deposition gives sclerenchyma cells extreme hardness and rigidity? *(Answer: Lignin)*
3. Differentiate between a Tendon and a Ligament. *(Answer: Tendon connects muscle to bone with high strength and limited flexibility; Ligament connects bone to bone with high elasticity)*
4. Which muscle tissue is branched, striated, and involuntary? *(Answer: Cardiac muscle)*
5. Name the fat-storing tissue found beneath the skin that acts as an insulator. *(Answer: Adipose tissue)*

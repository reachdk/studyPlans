## 3. Historical Models of the Atom

### A. J.J. Thomson's Model (Plum Pudding / Watermelon)
* **Postulate**: An atom consists of a positively charged sphere in which negatively charged electrons are embedded like seeds in a watermelon or raisins in a pudding.
* **Success**: Explained the electrical neutrality of atoms.
* **Drawback**: Failed to explain how positive and negative charges stay separated or the results of Rutherford's scattering experiments.

### B. Rutherford's $\alpha$-Particle Scattering Experiment (Gold Foil)
* **Setup**: Fast-moving doubly-charged Helium ions ($\alpha$-particles, $\text{He}^{2+}$, mass $4\text{ u}$) directed at an ultra-thin gold foil ($\sim 1000$ atoms thick) surrounded by a circular fluorescent $\text{ZnS}$ detector screen.
* **Observations & Conclusions**:
  1. *Most $\alpha$-particles passed straight through undeflected* $\rightarrow$ **Most of the space inside an atom is empty**.
  2. *A small fraction deflected through small angles* $\rightarrow$ **The positive charge occupies very little volume inside the atom**.
  3. *A tiny fraction ($\approx 1 \text{ in } 12,000$) rebounded back ($180^\circ$)* $\rightarrow$ **All positive charge and nearly all mass of the atom is concentrated in an extremely dense, compact central core called the Nucleus**.
* **Major Drawback**: According to classical physics, an accelerating electron revolving around a nucleus in a curved path should continuously radiate energy. Losing energy, it would spiral inwards and collapse into the nucleus within $\sim 10^{-8}\text{ s}$. Rutherford's model could not explain atomic stability.

### C. Neils Bohr's Model of the Atom (1913)
To overcome Rutherford's instability drawback, Bohr postulated:
1. Electrons revolve only in certain **discrete, non-radiating orbits** called **stationary energy levels** or **shells**.
2. While revolving in these discrete orbits, electrons **do not radiate energy**.
3. Energy levels are designated by integers $n = 1, 2, 3, 4\dots$ or letters **K, L, M, N...** shells from inner to outer.

---

## 4. Bohr-Bury Scheme: Electron Distribution in Shells

1. **Maximum capacity of shell $n$**: $2n^2$
   * **K Shell ($n=1$):** $2(1)^2 = \mathbf{2}$ electrons max.
   * **L Shell ($n=2$):** $2(2)^2 = \mathbf{8}$ electrons max.
   * **M Shell ($n=3$):** $2(3)^2 = \mathbf{18}$ electrons max (stable octet holds 8 before filling N for first 20 elements).
   * **N Shell ($n=4$):** $2(4)^2 = \mathbf{32}$ electrons max.
2. **Outermost Shell (Octet Rule)**: The outermost valence shell cannot accommodate more than **8 electrons**, regardless of shell capacity.
3. Electrons do not enter a new outer shell until the inner shells are completely filled (stepwise filling).

## 5. Valence Electrons & Valency (Elements 1 to 20)

* **Valence Electrons**: Number of electrons present in the outermost shell.
* **Valency**: The combining capacity of an atom to achieve a stable octet (or duplet for H/He).
  * If valence electrons $\le 4$: $\mathbf{\text{Valency} = \text{Number of Valence Electrons}}$
  * If valence electrons $> 4$: $\mathbf{\text{Valency} = 8 - \text{Number of Valence Electrons}}$

| Element | Symbol | Atomic No. ($Z$) | Electronic Configuration (K, L, M) | Valence Electrons | Valency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hydrogen | $\text{H}$ | 1 | 1 | 1 | **1** |
| Helium | $\text{He}$ | 2 | 2 | 2 | **0** (Stable duplet) |
| Carbon | $\text{C}$ | 6 | 2, 4 | 4 | **4** |
| Nitrogen | $\text{N}$ | 7 | 2, 5 | 5 | $8 - 5 = \mathbf{3}$ |
| Oxygen | $\text{O}$ | 8 | 2, 6 | 6 | $8 - 6 = \mathbf{2}$ |
| Neon | $\text{Ne}$ | 10 | 2, 8 | 8 | $8 - 8 = \mathbf{0}$ (Inert octet) |
| Sodium | $\text{Na}$ | 11 | 2, 8, 1 | 1 | **1** |
| Magnesium| $\text{Mg}$| 12 | 2, 8, 2 | 2 | **2** |
| Aluminium| $\text{Al}$| 13 | 2, 8, 3 | 3 | **3** |
| Chlorine | $\text{Cl}$| 17 | 2, 8, 7 | 7 | $8 - 7 = \mathbf{1}$ |
| Argon | $\text{Ar}$| 18 | 2, 8, 8 | 8 | **0** |
| Calcium | $\text{Ca}$| 20 | 2, 8, 8, 2 | 2 | **2** |

## 6. Atomic Number, Mass Number, Isotopes & Isobars

* **Atomic Number ($Z$)**: Number of protons in the nucleus of an atom ($Z = p = e$ in a neutral atom).
* **Mass Number ($A$)**: Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus ($A = p + n$).
* **Symbol Notation**: $${}_Z^A\text{X} \quad \text{e.g. } {}_{11}^{23}\text{Na} \implies Z = 11, A = 23, n = 23 - 11 = 12$$

### Isotopes vs. Isobars:
| Feature | **Isotopes** | **Isobars** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Atoms of the **same element** with same $Z$ but different $A$. | Atoms of **different elements** with different $Z$ but same $A$. |
| **Protons ($Z$)** | Same | Different |
| **Neutrons ($n$)**| Different | Different |
| **Chemical Properties**| Identical (same electron configuration) | Different (different elements) |
| **Physical Properties**| Different (different masses) | Different |
| **Examples** | ${}_1^1\text{H}$ (Protium), ${}_1^2\text{H}$ (Deuterium), ${}_1^3\text{H}$ (Tritium); ${}_{17}^{35}\text{Cl}$ and ${}_{17}^{37}\text{Cl}$ | ${}_{18}^{40}\text{Ar}$ and ${}_{20}^{40}\text{Ca}$ |

### Average Atomic Mass of Chlorine:
Chlorine exists as ${}_{17}^{35}\text{Cl}$ ($75\%$) and ${}_{17}^{37}\text{Cl}$ ($25\%$):
$$\text{Average Mass} = \left(35 \times \frac{75}{100}\right) + \left(37 \times \frac{25}{100}\right) = 26.25 + 9.25 = \mathbf{35.5\text{ u}}$$

### Crucial Applications of Radioactive Isotopes:
1. **Cobalt-60 (${}^{60}\text{Co}$)**: In radiotherapy for the treatment of **cancer**.
2. **Iodine-131 (${}^{131}\text{I}$)**: In the treatment of **goitre** (thyroid diseases).
3. **Uranium-235 (${}^{235}\text{U}$)**: As fuel in **nuclear reactors** for electricity generation.

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Who discovered the neutron, and what is its charge? *(Answer: James Chadwick in 1932; Neutral / zero charge)*
2. What are the maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the M shell ($n=3$)? *(Answer: $2(3)^2 = 18$ electrons)*
3. What is the valency of Nitrogen ($Z = 7$)? *(Answer: 3, because configuration is 2, 5, so valency = $8 - 5 = 3$)*
4. Why do isotopes of an element have identical chemical properties? *(Answer: Because they have identical atomic numbers and identical valence electron configurations, which determine chemical reactivity)*
5. Which radioactive isotope is used in the treatment of cancer? *(Answer: Cobalt-60)*

## 2. Laws of Chemical Combination

### 1. Law of Conservation of Mass (Antoine Lavoisier)
> *"Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction."*
* **Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products**.
* *Verification experiment*: Reaction between aqueous Barium Chloride ($\text{BaCl}_2$) and Sodium Sulphate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$) in a sealed conical flask produces a white precipitate of Barium Sulphate ($\text{BaSO}_4$) and Sodium Chloride ($\text{NaCl}$); the mass measured before and after mixing remains precisely unchanged.

### 2. Law of Constant / Definite Proportions (Joseph Proust)
> *"In a chemical substance, the elements are always present in definite proportions by mass, regardless of source or method of preparation."*
* *Example 1 (Water, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$)*: Hydrogen and Oxygen combine in a fixed mass ratio of **$1 : 8$**. Decomposing $9\text{ g}$ of pure water always yields $1\text{ g}$ of Hydrogen gas and $8\text{ g}$ of Oxygen gas.
* *Example 2 (Ammonia, $\text{NH}_3$)*: Nitrogen and Hydrogen are always combined in the mass ratio of **$14 : 3$**.
* *Example 3 (Carbon Dioxide, $\text{CO}_2$)*: Carbon and Oxygen combine in the mass ratio of **$3 : 8$** ($12\text{ g C} : 32\text{ g O}$).

## 3. Dalton's Atomic Theory

1. All matter is made up of very tiny, indivisible particles called **atoms**.
2. Atoms cannot be created or destroyed in a chemical reaction (*explains Law of Conservation of Mass*).
3. Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
4. Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties.
5. Atoms combine in the ratio of small whole numbers to form compounds (*explains Law of Definite Proportions*).
6. The relative number and kinds of atoms are constant in a given compound.

## 4. Atoms, Atomic Mass Unit & Atomicity

* **Atomic Radius**: Measured in nanometres ($\text{nm}$). $1\text{ m} = 10^9\text{ nm} \implies 1\text{ nm} = 10^{-9}\text{ m}$.
  * Radius of Hydrogen atom $\approx 10^{-10}\text{ m} = 0.1\text{ nm}$.
* **Unified Atomic Mass Unit ($\text{u}$)**:
  * By international standard, $1\text{ u}$ is defined as a mass unit equal to exactly **$\frac{1}{12}\text{th}$ the mass of one atom of Carbon-12 ($^{12}\text{C}$)**.
* **Atomicity**: The number of atoms constituting a single molecule of an element or compound.

| Element | Molecule | Atomicity | Classification |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Helium ($\text{He}$), Argon ($\text{Ar}$) | $\text{He}$, $\text{Ar}$ | 1 | **Monoatomic** (Noble gases) |
| Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$), Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$), Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$) | $\text{H}_2$, $\text{O}_2$, $\text{N}_2$ | 2 | **Diatomic** |
| Ozone ($\text{O}_3$) | $\text{O}_3$ | 3 | **Triatomic** |
| Phosphorus ($\text{P}_4$) | $\text{P}_4$ | 4 | **Tetra-atomic** |
| Sulphur ($\text{S}_8$) | $\text{S}_8$ | 8 | **Polyatomic** |

## 5. Ions and Polyatomic Radicals (Essential Valency Table)

* **Cation**: Positively charged ion formed by losing electrons (e.g. $\text{Na}^+, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Al}^{3+}$).
* **Anion**: Negatively charged ion formed by gaining electrons (e.g. $\text{Cl}^-, \text{O}^{2-}, \text{N}^{3-}$).
* **Polyatomic Ion**: A group of atoms carrying a net positive or negative electric charge.

| Valency | Cations ($+$) | Anions ($-$) | Polyatomic Ions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | $\text{H}^+, \text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Ag}^+, \text{Cu}^+$ | $\text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-, \text{H}^-$ (Hydride) | $\text{NH}_4^+$ (Ammonium), $\text{OH}^-$ (Hydroxide), $\text{NO}_3^-$ (Nitrate), $\text{HCO}_3^-$ (Hydrogen Carbonate) |
| **2** | $\text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{2+}, \text{Cu}^{2+}$ | $\text{O}^{2-}$ (Oxide), $\text{S}^{2-}$ (Sulphide) | $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ (Carbonate), $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ (Sulphate), $\text{SO}_3^{2-}$ (Sulphite) |
| **3** | $\text{Al}^{3+}, \text{Fe}^{3+}$ | $\text{N}^{3-}$ (Nitride) | $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ (Phosphate) |

## 6. Writing Chemical Formulae (The Criss-Cross Rule)

1. Write the symbols of constituent elements/ions side by side (positive metal/cation on the left, negative non-metal/anion on the right).
2. Write the valency of each radical below its symbol.
3. Cross-over the valencies to form subscripts.
4. Simplify subscripts to lowest whole numbers. If a polyatomic ion has a subscript $> 1$, enclose it in parentheses.

### Examples:
* **Aluminium Oxide**:
  $$\text{Symbols: } \text{Al} \quad \text{O} \quad \Big| \quad \text{Valencies: } 3 \quad 2 \implies \mathbf{\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3}$$
* **Calcium Hydroxide**:
  $$\text{Symbols: } \text{Ca} \quad \text{OH} \quad \Big| \quad \text{Valencies: } 2 \quad 1 \implies \mathbf{\text{Ca(OH)}_2}$$
* **Ammonium Sulphate**:
  $$\text{Symbols: } \text{NH}_4 \quad \text{SO}_4 \quad \Big| \quad \text{Valencies: } 1 \quad 2 \implies \mathbf{(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4}$$
* **Magnesium Chloride**:
  $$\text{Symbols: } \text{Mg} \quad \text{Cl} \quad \Big| \quad \text{Valencies: } 2 \quad 1 \implies \mathbf{\text{MgCl}_2}$$

## 7. Molecular Mass & Formula Unit Mass

The sum of the atomic masses of all the atoms in a molecule or ionic formula unit:
* Standard atomic masses to know: $\text{H} = 1\text{ u}$, $\text{C} = 12\text{ u}$, $\text{N} = 14\text{ u}$, $\text{O} = 16\text{ u}$, $\text{Na} = 23\text{ u}$, $\text{Mg} = 24\text{ u}$, $\text{S} = 32\text{ u}$, $\text{Cl} = 35.5\text{ u}$, $\text{Ca} = 40\text{ u}$.

### Worked Examples:
1. **Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**:
   $$(2 \times 1) + (1 \times 16) = \mathbf{18\text{ u}}$$
2. **Nitric Acid ($\text{HNO}_3$)**:
   $$(1 \times 1) + (1 \times 14) + (3 \times 16) = 1 + 14 + 48 = \mathbf{63\text{ u}}$$
3. **Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$)**:
   $$(1 \times 40) + (1 \times 12) + (3 \times 16) = 40 + 12 + 48 = \mathbf{100\text{ u}}$$

## 8. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. State the Law of Constant Proportions. *(Answer: In a chemical substance, the elements are always present in definite proportions by mass)*
2. What is the atomicity of Phosphorus and Ozone? *(Answer: Phosphorus = 4 / Tetra-atomic; Ozone = 3 / Triatomic)*
3. Write the chemical formula of Sodium Carbonate and Calcium Oxide. *(Answer: $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ and $\text{CaO}$)*
4. Calculate the molecular mass of Glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$). *(Answer: $(6 \times 12) + (12 \times 1) + (6 \times 16) = 72 + 12 + 96 = 180\text{ u}$)*
5. How is $1\text{ u}$ defined? *(Answer: Exactly $\frac{1}{12}\text{th}$ of the mass of one Carbon-12 atom)*

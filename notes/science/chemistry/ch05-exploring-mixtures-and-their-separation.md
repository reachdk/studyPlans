## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Pure Substances vs. Mixtures**: Elements, compounds, homogeneous vs. heterogeneous mixtures.
* **True Solution, Colloid & Suspension**: Core definitions, particle sizes, optical properties (Tyndall effect), and physical stability.
* **Concentration of a Solution**: Saturated vs. unsaturated solutions, solubility, mass percentage ($\% \text{ w/w}$) and volume percentage ($\% \text{ v/v}$).
* **Colloids**: Dispersed phase and dispersion medium; classification into sols, gels, emulsions, aerosols, and foams.
* **Separation Techniques**:
  * Evaporation (volatile solvent from non-volatile solute)
  * Centrifugation (density-based separation of blood/cream)
  * Separating Funnel (immiscible liquids like oil and water)
  * Sublimation (camphor/ammonium chloride from salt)
  * Chromatography (separation of dyes/pigments by differential solubility)
  * Distillation & Fractional Distillation (miscible liquids with boiling point differences $> 25\text{ K}$ or $< 25\text{ K}$)
  * Crystallisation (pure solid crystals from impure solution).
* **Physical vs. Chemical Changes**: Reversible physical state changes vs. irreversible composition-altering reactions.

## 3. Comparison: True Solution vs. Colloid vs. Suspension

| Property | True Solution (e.g. Salt in Water) | Colloid (e.g. Milk, Fog, Ink) | Suspension (e.g. Chalk in Water, Muddy Water) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type of Mixture** | Homogeneous | Heterogeneous (appears homogeneous) | Heterogeneous |
| **Particle Size** | $< 1\text{ nm}$ ($< 10^{-9}\text{ m}$) | $1\text{ nm}$ to $100\text{ nm}$ | $> 100\text{ nm}$ ($> 10^{-7}\text{ m}$) |
| **Filterability** | Passes through ordinary and ultra filter paper | Passes through filter paper; separated only by centrifugation | Retained on ordinary filter paper |
| **Stability** | Completely stable (particles never settle) | Stable (does not settle on standing) | Unstable (particles settle under gravity) |
| **Tyndall Effect** | **Does not scatter light** (Path of light invisible) | **Scatters light** (Shows distinct Tyndall effect) | Scatters light initially until particles settle |
| **Visibility** | Invisible under any optical microscope | Visible only under electron microscope | Visible to naked eyes or magnifying lens |

## 4. Concentration of a Solution

* **Solute**: Substance dissolved in smaller proportion (e.g. salt).
* **Solvent**: Dissolving medium in larger proportion (e.g. water).
* **Saturated Solution**: A solution in which no more solute can be dissolved at a given temperature.
* **Solubility**: The maximum mass of solute in grams that can dissolve in $100\text{ g}$ of solvent at a specific temperature to form a saturated solution.

### Mathematical Formulas for Concentration:
1. **Mass by Mass Percentage ($\% \text{ w/w}$)**:
   $$\text{Mass } \% = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \times 100 = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solute} + \text{Mass of Solvent}} \times 100$$
2. **Mass by Volume Percentage ($\% \text{ w/v}$)**:
   $$\text{Mass by Volume } \% = \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \times 100$$

> [!WARNING]
> **Denominator Trap**: Remember that the denominator is the **Mass of Solution**, which is Solute + Solvent! If $40\text{ g}$ of salt is added to $320\text{ g}$ of water, total mass of solution is $40 + 320 = 360\text{ g}$, so concentration is $\frac{40}{360} \times 100 = 11.1\%$.

## 5. Master Guide to Separation Techniques

| Mixture to Separate | Property Difference Exploited | Best Technique | Real-World Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dye from Blue/Black Ink** | Volatility of solvent (water) vs non-volatile dye solute | **Evaporation** | Obtaining colored component of ink. |
| **Cream from Milk / Blood cells from plasma** | Density differences under rapid rotation | **Centrifugation** | Diagnostic labs (blood & urine tests); dairies for butter making; washing machines to squeeze water out of clothes. |
| **Kerosene and Water (Immiscible liquids)** | Liquids do not mix and form distinct density layers | **Separating Funnel** | Extracting oil from water; molten slag separation in blast furnaces. |
| **Camphor + Salt / $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$ + Salt** | One substance sublimates directly (solid $\rightarrow$ gas) while other does not | **Sublimation** | Purifying camphor, naphthalene, anthracene, ammonium chloride. |
| **Different colors/pigments in black ink or leaf extract** | Differential rates of adsorption and solubility in solvent | **Chromatography** | Separating dyes in ink, natural pigments in flowers, drugs from blood. |
| **Acetone + Water (Miscible, $\Delta \text{BP} > 25\text{ K}$)** | Large difference in boiling points | **Simple Distillation** | Separating miscible liquids with boiling point difference $> 25^\circ\text{C}$. |
| **Petroleum fractions / Air gases ($\Delta \text{BP} < 25\text{ K}$)** | Small difference in boiling points; uses fractionating column packed with glass beads | **Fractional Distillation** | Refining crude petroleum into petrol, diesel, kerosene; separating nitrogen ($196^\circ\text{C}$), argon ($-186^\circ\text{C}$), and oxygen ($-183^\circ\text{C}$) from liquid air. |
| **Pure Copper Sulphate from Impure Sample** | Solute crystallizes out from hot saturated solution on cooling | **Crystallisation** | Purifying alum (phitkari), salt from sea water, copper sulphate crystals. (Superior to evaporation because it prevents thermal decomposition). |

## 6. Physical vs. Chemical Changes

* **Physical Change**:
  * Changes only physical state, size, or appearance.
  * No new chemical substance is formed; chemically reversible.
  * *Examples*: Melting of ice, boiling of water, dissolving sugar in water, tearing paper, melting of candle wax.
* **Chemical Change**:
  * Alters the chemical composition and creates entirely new chemical substances with distinct properties.
  * Generally irreversible; accompanied by heat/light absorption or release.
  * *Examples*: Rusting of iron, burning of paper/wood, cooking food, digestion, burning of a candle (the burning of the wick and wax vapor is chemical, while the melting of solid wax is physical).

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Why is a mixture of water and milk considered heterogeneous even though it appears uniform? *(Answer: Under a microscope, fat globules remain suspended as distinct droplets; it also scatters light displaying the Tyndall effect)*
2. What technique would you use to separate a mixture of acetone (B.P. $56^\circ\text{C}$) and water (B.P. $100^\circ\text{C}$)? *(Answer: Simple Distillation, because their boiling point difference is $44^\circ\text{C} > 25^\circ\text{C}$)*
3. Why is crystallisation considered a better purification technique than simple evaporation to dryness? *(Answer: Some solids decompose or get charred upon heating to dryness, and soluble impurities remain in the solid during evaporation but stay in solution during crystallisation)*
4. Calculate the concentration of a solution prepared by dissolving $30\text{ g}$ of sugar in $170\text{ g}$ of water. *(Answer: $\text{Mass } \% = \frac{30}{30 + 170} \times 100 = \frac{30}{200} \times 100 = 15\%$)*
5. Give one example of a solid-in-liquid colloid. *(Answer: Milk of magnesia, mud)*

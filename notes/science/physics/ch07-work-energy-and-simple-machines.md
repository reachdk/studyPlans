## 2. Scientific Work Done

### When is Work Done in Physics?
Two essential conditions must **both** be satisfied for work to be done:
1. A force must act on the object.
2. The object must be displaced along or in the direction of the force.

> [!NOTE]
> Even if a student pushes hard against a rigid concrete wall for hours until exhausted, in Physics, **Work Done = 0** because displacement $s = 0$.

### Mathematical Formula:
$$W = F \times s$$
* Where $F$ is force ($\text{N}$) and $s$ is displacement ($\text{m}$).
* **SI Unit of Work**: **Joule ($\text{J}$)** or Newton-metre ($\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$).
* **Definition of 1 Joule**: The amount of work done on an object when a force of $1\text{ N}$ displaces it by $1\text{ m}$ in the direction of the force ($1\text{ J} = 1\text{ N} \times 1\text{ m}$).

### Types of Work Done:
| Type | Angle ($\theta$) | Formula | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive Work** | $\theta = 0^\circ$ (Force & displacement in **same direction**) | $W = +F \cdot s$ | A horse pulling a cart forward; gravity doing work on a falling apple. |
| **Negative Work** | $\theta = 180^\circ$ (Force opposes displacement) | $W = -F \cdot s$ | Work done by **friction** when a moving box slides to a halt; work done by gravity on a ball thrown upwards. |
| **Zero Work** | $\theta = 90^\circ$ (Force is **perpendicular** to displacement) or $s = 0$ | $W = 0$ | A porter carrying a load on his head walking on horizontal ground (gravity acts downward at $90^\circ$ to horizontal displacement); Earth orbiting the Sun (centripetal force perpendicular to orbital velocity). |

## 3. Energy: Forms & Derivations

* **Definition**: Energy is the capacity or capability of a body to do work.
* **SI Unit**: **Joule ($\text{J}$)** (Same as work). $1\text{ kJ} = 1000\text{ J}$.

### A. Kinetic Energy ($E_k$)
The energy possessed by a body by virtue of its **motion**.
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

#### Derivation:
1. From 3rd equation of motion: $v^2 - u^2 = 2as \implies s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}$
2. Work done: $W = F \times s = (ma) \times \left(\frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}\right) = \frac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2)$
3. If the body starts from rest ($u = 0$), then $W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
4. Since work done is stored as kinetic energy:
   $$\mathbf{E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2}$$

### B. Gravitational Potential Energy ($E_p$)
The energy possessed by a body by virtue of its **position or configuration** (height above ground).
$$E_p = mgh$$
* *Where*: $m$ = mass ($\text{kg}$), $g$ = acceleration due to gravity ($\approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ or $10\text{ m/s}^2$), $h$ = vertical height ($\text{m}$).

---

## 4. Law of Conservation of Energy

> *"Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form into another. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant."*

### Mechanical Energy in Free Fall:
Consider an object of mass $m$ dropped from height $H$:
* **At Top (Point A, height $H$):**
  * Velocity $u = 0 \implies E_k = 0$
  * $E_p = mgH$
  * **Total Energy** $= mgH$
* **Midway (Point B, fallen by $x$, height $H - x$):**
  * $v^2 = 0 + 2gx = 2gx \implies E_k = \frac{1}{2}m(2gx) = mgx$
  * $E_p = mg(H - x) = mgH - mgx$
  * **Total Energy** $= mgx + (mgH - mgx) = \mathbf{mgH}$
* **Just Before Hitting Ground (Point C, height $0$):**
  * $v^2 = 2gH \implies E_k = \frac{1}{2}m(2gH) = mgH$
  * $E_p = 0$
  * **Total Energy** $= \mathbf{mgH}$

> As potential energy decreases, kinetic energy increases by the exact same amount: **Total Mechanical Energy ($E_k + E_p$) is conserved.**

## 5. Power & Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy

* **Power ($P$)**: The rate of doing work or rate of energy consumption.
  $$P = \frac{\text{Work Done}}{\text{Time Taken}} = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{E}{t}$$
* **SI Unit**: **Watt ($\text{W}$)** ($1\text{ W} = 1\text{ Joule/second}$).
* Larger units: $1\text{ Kilowatt (kW)} = 1000\text{ W}$; $1\text{ Horsepower (hp)} = 746\text{ W}$.

### Commercial Unit of Energy (Kilowatt-Hour, $\text{kWh}$):
* Electricity bills measure energy in **Units** ($1\text{ Unit} = 1\text{ kWh}$).
* **Conversion from $\text{kWh}$ to Joules**:
  $$1\text{ kWh} = 1\text{ kW} \times 1\text{ hour} = 1000\text{ W} \times 3600\text{ s} = \mathbf{3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}}$$

## 6. Simple Machines (Overview)
* **Mechanical Advantage (MA)**: $\text{MA} = \frac{\text{Load}}{\text{Effort}}$
* **Velocity Ratio (VR)**: $\text{VR} = \frac{\text{Distance moved by Effort}}{\text{Distance moved by Load}}$
* **Efficiency ($\eta$)**: $\eta = \frac{\text{Work Output}}{\text{Work Input}} = \frac{\text{MA}}{\text{VR}} \times 100\%$ (Always $< 100\%$ due to friction).

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. What is the work done by the gravitational force on an artificial satellite revolving around Earth in a circular orbit? *(Answer: Zero, because gravitational pull is perpendicular to the satellite's orbital displacement at every point)*
2. If the speed of a car is tripled ($3v$), by what factor does its kinetic energy increase? *(Answer: 9 times, because $E_k \propto v^2$)*
3. A body of mass $2\text{ kg}$ is dropped from a height of $5\text{ m}$. Calculate its kinetic energy just before striking the ground ($g = 10\text{ m/s}^2$). *(Answer: $E_k = mgh = 2 \times 10 \times 5 = 100\text{ J}$)*
4. Express $1\text{ kWh}$ in Joules. *(Answer: $3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}$)*
5. What is the power of an appliance that consumes $1000\text{ J}$ of energy in $2\text{ seconds}$? *(Answer: $P = \frac{1000}{2} = 500\text{ W}$)*

## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Production & Propagation of Sound**: Vibrating objects create sound; sound is a mechanical wave requiring a material medium (cannot travel in vacuum).
* **Wave Nature**: Longitudinal vs. transverse waves; compressions ($C$) and rarefactions ($R$).
* **Characteristics of Sound Waves**: Wavelength ($\lambda$), Frequency ($\nu$), Time Period ($T$), Amplitude ($A$), Speed ($v = \nu\lambda$).
* **Sensory Qualities**: Pitch (frequency), Loudness (amplitude), Timber/Quality (waveform).

* **Reflection of Sound**: Laws of reflection, Echo calculation (minimum distance $17.2\text{ m}$ in air at $22^\circ\text{C}$), Reverberation and acoustic control.
* **Audible Range & Infrasound / Ultrasound**: Applications in medicine (ultrasonography, echocardiography), industry (flaw detection), and navigation (**SONAR**).

## 2. Core Concepts & Wave Mechanics

### Production & Medium:
* Sound is produced by **vibrating bodies** (tuning fork prongs, vocal cords, stretched strings, vibrating air columns).
* Sound travels as a **mechanical wave**: it transfers energy through periodic compressions and rarefactions of medium particles without displacing the medium as a whole.
* **Sound cannot travel through vacuum**: Proved by the classic **Bell-Jar Experiment** (as air is evacuated, sound fades to silence).

### Longitudinal vs. Transverse Waves:
| Feature | Longitudinal Waves (e.g. Sound in Air) | Transverse Waves (e.g. Light, Waves on Water) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Particle Oscillation** | Parallel to the direction of wave propagation | Perpendicular ($90^\circ$) to wave propagation |
| **Components** | **Compressions ($C$)** (high density/pressure) and **Rarefactions ($R$)** (low density/pressure) | **Crests** (peaks) and **Troughs** (valleys) |
| **Medium Requirement** | Must have material medium with elasticity | Can travel through vacuum (for electromagnetic waves) |

## 3. Wave Parameters & Key Equations

1. **Wavelength ($\lambda$)**: The distance between two consecutive compressions or two consecutive rarefactions.
   * *SI Unit*: Metre ($\text{m}$).
2. **Frequency ($\nu$ or $f$)**: The number of complete wave oscillations passing a point per unit time.
   * *SI Unit*: **Hertz ($\text{Hz}$)** ($1\text{ Hz} = 1\text{ cycle/second}$).
3. **Time Period ($T$)**: The time taken by a particle to complete one full oscillation.
   * *Relation*: $$T = \frac{1}{\nu} \iff \nu = \frac{1}{T}$$
4. **Amplitude ($A$)**: The maximum magnitude of displacement of the medium particles from their mean equilibrium position.
   * Determines **Loudness**: $\text{Loudness} \propto \text{Amplitude}^2$.
5. **Wave Equation (Master Formula)**:
   $$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} = \frac{\lambda}{T} \implies \mathbf{v = \nu \lambda}$$
   $$\text{Velocity } (v) = \text{Frequency } (\nu) \times \text{Wavelength } (\lambda)$$

## 4. Characteristics of Sound: Pitch, Loudness & Quality

* **Pitch (Shrillness vs. Flatness)**:
  * Determined strictly by **Frequency**.
  * High frequency $\rightarrow$ High pitch (shrill, e.g. whistle, bird chirping, female voice).
  * Low frequency $\rightarrow$ Low pitch (grave/deep, e.g. drum beat, lion roar, male voice).
* **Loudness (Volume)**:
  * Determined strictly by **Amplitude**.
  * Large amplitude $\rightarrow$ Louder sound (measured in Decibels, $\text{dB}$).
* **Timber / Quality**:
  * The characteristic that enables us to distinguish between two sounds having the same pitch and loudness produced by different musical instruments (determined by harmonic overtones and wave shape).
  * Single frequency sound = **Tone**; mixture of several frequencies = **Note**.

## 5. Reflection of Sound: Echo, Reverberation & SONAR

### A. Echo
* An echo is the distinct repetition of sound caused by reflection from a distant obstacle (cliff, tall building, mountain).
* **Persistence of Hearing**: The sensation of sound persists in the human brain for about **$0.1\text{ s}$ ($\frac{1}{10}\text{ s}$)**. To hear a distinct echo, the reflected sound must reach the ear after at least $0.1\text{ s}$.
* **Minimum Distance for Echo in Air at $22^\circ\text{C}$ ($v = 344\text{ m/s}$)**:
  $$\text{Total 2-way path } 2d = v \times t = 344 \times 0.1 = 34.4\text{ m}$$
  $$\mathbf{d_{\text{min}} = \frac{34.4}{2} = 17.2\text{ m}}$$

### B. Reverberation
* The persistence of sound in an enclosed hall resulting from **repeated multiple reflections** from ceilings and walls until it is absorbed.
* Excessive reverberation causes speech to become blurred and unintelligible.
* **How to Reduce Reverberation**: Cover walls and ceilings with sound-absorbent materials (perforated acoustic boards, heavy draperies/curtains, compressed fiberboard, cushioned seats).

### C. SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging)
* Uses ultrasound pulses to measure the depth of the seabed or locate underwater objects (submarines, shipwrecks, icebergs).
* If a transmitter sends an ultrasound pulse that is reflected from the seabed and detected by a receiver after time $t$:
  $$2d = v \times t \implies \mathbf{d = \frac{v \times t}{2}}$$

## 6. Audible, Infrasonic & Ultrasonic Sound

* **Human Audible Range**: $20\text{ Hz}$ to $20,000\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ kHz}$).
* **Infrasonic Sound**: Frequencies **below $20\text{ Hz}$**.
  * Produced by: Earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, rhinoceroses ($5\text{ Hz}$), elephants, whales.
* **Ultrasonic Sound (Ultrasound)**: Frequencies **above $20,000\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ kHz}$)**.
  * Used by: Bats (echolocation navigation and hunting), dolphins, porpoises.
  * **Medical / Industrial Applications**:
    1. **Echocardiography (ECG)**: Ultrasonic imaging of the beating heart.
    2. **Ultrasonography**: Monitoring prenatal foetus development and examining internal organs.
    3. **Industrial Non-Destructive Testing**: Detecting invisible hairline cracks or air voids in metallic blocks and bridges.
    4. **Kidney Stone Lithotripsy**: High-energy ultrasound breaks kidney stones into fine sand grains flushed out through urine.

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. Why can sound not be heard on the Moon? *(Answer: The Moon has no atmosphere/medium, and sound waves require a material medium to propagate)*
2. What wave property determines the pitch of a sound? *(Answer: Frequency)*
3. What is the minimum distance between a source of sound and a reflecting surface to hear an echo at $22^\circ\text{C}$? *(Answer: $17.2\text{ m}$)*
4. If a sound wave has a frequency of $2\text{ kHz}$ ($2000\text{ Hz}$) and a wavelength of $35\text{ cm}$ ($0.35\text{ m}$), calculate its speed. *(Answer: $v = \nu\lambda = 2000 \times 0.35 = 700\text{ m/s}$)*
5. What is the frequency range of ultrasound? *(Answer: Greater than $20,000\text{ Hz}$ / $20\text{ kHz}$)*

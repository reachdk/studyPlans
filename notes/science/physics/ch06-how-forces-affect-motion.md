## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Balanced vs. Unbalanced Forces**: How net force determines changes in state of motion or shape.
* **Newton's First Law of Motion (Inertia)**: Inertia of rest, inertia of motion, and inertia of direction; mass as a quantitative measure of inertia.
* **Newton's Second Law of Motion (Momentum & Force)**: Linear momentum ($p = mv$), rate of change of momentum, derivation of $F = ma$, SI unit of force (Newton).
* **Newton's Third Law of Motion (Action & Reaction)**: Equal and opposite simultaneous forces acting on two *different* interacting bodies.
* **Conservation of Momentum**: In an isolated system (no external unbalanced force), total initial momentum equals total final momentum ($m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2$).
* **Practical Applications**: Catching a cricket ball, recoil of a gun, seat belts, rocket propulsion.

## 2. Core Concepts & Definitions

| Term | Scientific Definition | Formula / Representation | SI Unit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Force** | A push or pull upon an object resulting from its interaction with another object. | $F = ma$ | Newton ($\text{N}$) or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$ |
| **Balanced Forces** | Forces whose resultant (vector sum) is zero. They cannot change velocity, but can change shape. | $\sum F = 0$ | — |
| **Unbalanced Forces** | Forces whose resultant is non-zero. They produce acceleration (change speed or direction). | $\sum F \neq 0$ | — |
| **Inertia** | The natural inherent property of a body to resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion. | Measured by **Mass** ($m$) | Kilogram ($\text{kg}$) |
| **Momentum ($p$)** | The quantity of motion possessed by a moving body, equal to the product of mass and velocity. | $p = mv$ | $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ |
| **Impulse / Force-Time Impact** | Change in momentum produced by a force acting over a duration of time. | $F \times \Delta t = \Delta p$ | $\text{N}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ |

## 3. Newton's Three Laws of Motion

### 1. First Law (Law of Inertia)
> *"An object remains in a state of rest or of uniform motion in a straight line unless compelled to change that state by an applied unbalanced external force."*

* **Types of Inertia**:
  1. **Inertia of Rest**: Tendency to remain at rest (e.g. dust particles fall off a shaken carpet; fruits detach when tree branches are shaken).
  2. **Inertia of Motion**: Tendency to continue moving at the same speed (e.g. passengers jerk forward when a bus applies sudden brakes; an athlete runs a few paces past the finish line).
  3. **Inertia of Direction**: Tendency to maintain direction (e.g. mud flies off tangentially from rotating wheels, requiring mudguards; passengers tilt outward when a car rounds a sharp bend).
* **Mass as Measure of Inertia**: A heavier object (e.g., a bowling ball or train) has greater inertia and requires a much larger force to change its motion than a light object (e.g., a tennis ball).

---

### 2. Second Law (Measurement of Force)
> *"The rate of change of momentum of an object is directly proportional to the applied unbalanced force and takes place in the direction in which the force acts."*

#### Mathematical Derivation of $F = ma$:
Let an object of mass $m$ move with initial velocity $u$. An external force $F$ acts for time $t$, changing its velocity to $v$.
1. Initial momentum: $p_1 = mu$
2. Final momentum: $p_2 = mv$
3. Change in momentum: $\Delta p = p_2 - p_1 = m(v - u)$
4. Rate of change of momentum: $\frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{m(v - u)}{t} = ma \quad (\text{since } a = \frac{v - u}{t})$
5. By Newton's Second Law:
   $$F \propto \frac{\Delta p}{t} \implies F = k \cdot ma$$
   In SI units, the constant of proportionality $k = 1$, yielding:
   $$\mathbf{F = ma}$$

* **Definition of 1 Newton**: The force that produces an acceleration of $1\text{ m/s}^2$ in a mass of $1\text{ kg}$ ($1\text{ N} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$).

---

### 3. Third Law (Action and Reaction)
> *"To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction."*

* **Crucial Rule**: Action and reaction forces **always act on two different bodies simultaneously**.
  * They **never cancel each other out** because they act on separate bodies.
* **Examples**:
  * **Walking**: Feet push ground backward (action); ground pushes feet forward with friction (reaction).
  * **Swimming**: Swimmer pushes water backward (action); water pushes swimmer forward (reaction).
  * **Recoil of Gun**: Gun pushes bullet forward with force $F$; bullet pushes gun backward with equal force $-F$.

## 4. Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum

> *"When two or more bodies act upon one another in an isolated system (no external unbalanced force), their total momentum remains constant."*

### Collision Formula:
For two bodies of masses $m_1$ and $m_2$, with initial velocities $u_1, u_2$ and final velocities $v_1, v_2$:
$$\mathbf{m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2}$$

### Recoil Velocity of a Gun:
Before firing, both gun and bullet are at rest (Total initial momentum = $0$).
After firing:
$$M_{\text{gun}} V_{\text{recoil}} + m_{\text{bullet}} v_{\text{bullet}} = 0$$
$$\mathbf{V_{\text{recoil}} = -\frac{m_{\text{bullet}} \times v_{\text{bullet}}}{M_{\text{gun}}}}$$
*(Negative sign indicates recoil velocity is opposite to the bullet's direction of fire).*

## 5. "Why Does This Happen?" (Scientific Reasoning)

1. **Why does a fielder pull their hands backward while catching a fast-moving cricket ball?**
   * *Reason*: By pulling hands backward, the fielder **increases the time interval ($\Delta t$)** taken to bring the ball's momentum to zero. Since $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, increasing the time reduces the rate of change of momentum, drastically reducing the impact force on the fielder's palms to prevent injury.
2. **Why does a karate player break a slab of ice or pile of tiles with a single swift blow?**
   * *Reason*: The karate player strikes with extremely high speed and brings their hand to rest in a **split second (very tiny $\Delta t$)**. This extremely short duration creates an enormous rate of change of momentum, exerting a massive impact force sufficient to shatter the tiles.
3. **Why are athletes in high-jump events made to land on a cushioned bed or sand pit?**
   * *Reason*: The cushion or loose sand deforms on impact, lengthening the time taken to bring the athlete's downward momentum to zero. This decreases the impact force, preventing bone fracture or injury.

## 6. Common Student Mistakes & Examiner Traps

> [!WARNING]
> * **Trap 1: Believing Action and Reaction cancel out**. Students often write "action and reaction cancel each other because they are equal and opposite." That is false! They can only cancel if they act on the same body. Since they act on **two different bodies**, motion occurs!
> * **Trap 2: Forgetting velocity direction signs in momentum problems**. Momentum is a vector! If a ball of mass $m$ strikes a wall at velocity $+u$ and rebounds at velocity $-v$, the change in momentum is $m(-v) - mu = -m(v + u)$, not $m(u - v)$!
> * **Trap 3: Recoil mass confusion**. The mass of the gun is much larger than the bullet, so its recoil velocity is much smaller, but the momentum of the gun is strictly equal and opposite to the momentum of the bullet.

## 7. Active Recall Checkpoint

1. What is the SI unit of momentum? *(Answer: $\text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$)*
2. What physical quantity is measured by the mass of a body? *(Answer: Inertia)*
3. If the force on a body is doubled while its mass remains constant, what happens to its acceleration? *(Answer: Acceleration is doubled, since $a \propto F$)*
4. Why does a gun recoil when fired? *(Answer: Due to conservation of momentum / Newton's 3rd Law: the bullet pushes the gun backward with equal and opposite momentum)*

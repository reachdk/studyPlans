## 1. Chapter Snapshot & Learning Objectives
* **Displacement vs. Distance**: Distinguish path length from vector position change.
* **Speed vs. Velocity**: Uniform and non-uniform motion, instantaneous vs. average velocity.
* **Acceleration**: Rate of change of velocity, positive and negative acceleration (retardation).
* **Graphical Analysis**: Interpret Distance-Time ($s-t$) and Velocity-Time ($v-t$) graphs; calculate speed/velocity from slope, and distance/displacement from area under $v-t$ curve.
* **Equations of Motion**: Understand and apply the three kinematic equations for uniformly accelerated motion ($v = u + at$, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$, $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$).
* **Uniform Circular Motion**: Speed is constant, but velocity continuously changes due to changing direction (accelerated motion).

---

## 2. Core Concepts & Definitions

| Term | Definition | Scalar or Vector? | SI Unit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reference Point (Origin)** | A fixed point chosen to specify the position of an object. | — | — |
| **Distance** | Total actual path length traversed by an object during motion. | Scalar (magnitude only) | Metre ($\text{m}$) |
| **Displacement** | The shortest straight-line directed distance from the initial position to the final position. | Vector (magnitude + direction) | Metre ($\text{m}$) |
| **Speed** | The distance travelled by an object per unit time ($v = s / t$). | Scalar | $\text{m/s}$ or $\text{km/h}$ |
| **Velocity** | The displacement of an object per unit time, or speed in a specified direction. | Vector | $\text{m/s}$ or $\text{km/h}$ |
| **Acceleration** | The rate of change of velocity with respect to time ($a = \frac{v - u}{t}$). | Vector | $\text{m/s}^2$ |

> [!NOTE]
> **Distance vs. Displacement Rule**:
> * Distance is **always positive** and **can never decrease** with time for a moving object.
> * Displacement can be **positive, negative, or zero** (e.g., returning to the starting point gives zero displacement, but non-zero distance).
> * **Magnitude of displacement $\le$ distance**. They are equal only when the object moves along a straight line in a single direction without turning back.

## 3. Key Formulas & Kinematic Equations

### A. Average Speed & Average Velocity
* **Average Speed**:
  $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance Travelled}}{\text{Total Time Taken}} = \frac{s_{\text{total}}}{t_{\text{total}}}$$
* **Average Velocity** (for uniformly changing velocity):
  $$v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{u + v}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad v_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Total Displacement}}{\text{Total Time}}$$

### B. Unit Conversion Rule
* From $\text{km/h}$ to $\text{m/s}$: multiply by $\frac{5}{18}$ (since $\frac{1000\text{ m}}{3600\text{ s}} = \frac{5}{18}$).
  * *Example*: $72\text{ km/h} = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20\text{ m/s}$.
* From $\text{m/s}$ to $\text{km/h}$: multiply by $\frac{18}{5}$.

### C. Three Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion
Applicable **only** when acceleration $a$ is constant along a straight line:

1. **Velocity-Time Relation**:
   $$v = u + at$$
2. **Position-Time Relation**:
   $$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
3. **Position-Velocity Relation**:
   $$v^2 - u^2 = 2as \quad (\text{or } v^2 = u^2 + 2as)$$

*Where:*
* $u$ = Initial velocity ($\text{m/s}$)
* $v$ = Final velocity ($\text{m/s}$)
* $a$ = Acceleration ($\text{m/s}^2$) [positive if speeding up, negative if decelerating/braking]
* $t$ = Time interval ($\text{s}$)
* $s$ = Distance / displacement covered ($\text{m}$)

### D. Uniform Circular Motion
When an object moves in a circular path of radius $r$ with constant speed:
$$v = \frac{2\pi r}{t}$$
* Even though the **speed is constant**, the **velocity is continuously changing** because the direction of motion at every instant is along the tangent to the circle.
* Therefore, uniform circular motion is an **accelerated motion**.

## 4. Graphical Representation of Motion

```
1. Distance-Time Graph (s vs. t):
   - Slope = Speed.
   - Straight line with positive slope → Uniform speed.
   - Curved line bending upwards → Increasing speed (accelerated).
   - Horizontal line parallel to time axis → Object is at rest (speed = 0).

2. Velocity-Time Graph (v vs. t):
   - Slope = Acceleration (a = Δv / Δt).
   - Area under the graph line & time axis = Distance / Displacement (s).
   - Horizontal line → Zero acceleration (constant velocity).
   - Straight line sloping upwards → Constant uniform positive acceleration.
   - Straight line sloping downwards → Constant uniform retardation / deceleration.
```

## 5. "Why Does This Happen?" (Scientific Reasoning)

1. **Why is circular motion considered accelerated even if the speedometer is constant?**
   * *Reason*: Velocity is a vector quantity having both magnitude and direction. In a circular path, the direction of motion continuously changes at every single point along the tangent. Because direction changes, velocity changes, and any change in velocity over time is acceleration.
2. **Can an object have zero velocity and still be accelerating?**
   * *Reason*: **Yes**. When a ball is thrown vertically upwards, at the highest point of its trajectory, its instantaneous velocity is momentarily zero ($v = 0$), but the acceleration due to gravity ($g \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ downwards) continues to act on it.
3. **Why does average velocity equal zero when an athlete runs one full lap around an oval track?**
   * *Reason*: The initial position and final position are identical, making the net displacement zero. Since $\text{Average Velocity} = \frac{\text{Net Displacement}}{\text{Time}}$, the average velocity is zero, even though the average speed is positive.

## 6. Common Student Mistakes & Examiner Traps

> [!WARNING]
> * **Trap 1: Confusing units in numericals**. Always check whether speed is in $\text{km/h}$ and time in $\text{seconds}$. Convert everything to SI units ($\text{m}$, $\text{s}$, $\text{m/s}$, $\text{m/s}^2$) before plugging into equations!
> * **Trap 2: Forgetting the negative sign for braking/retardation**. When brakes are applied, acceleration is opposing motion, so $a$ must be entered as a negative number (e.g. $a = -2\text{ m/s}^2$).
> * **Trap 3: Area under v-t graph calculation**. Remember to break compound areas into rectangles ($\text{base} \times \text{height}$) and triangles ($\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$) carefully when finding total distance.
